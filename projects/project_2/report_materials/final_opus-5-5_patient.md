# Marguerite Okonjo-Hale

## Background
Marguerite is 58 and spent thirty-one years as a land surveyor before retiring early when walking uneven terrain became unbearable. Her lower back pain began after a minor fall from a stile six years ago. Since then she has seen four GPs, two physiotherapists, and one orthopaedic consultant. All of them called the imaging "unremarkable for her age." She lives with her brother in a converted bungalow and keeps her symptom records in a series of numbered Moleskine notebooks, currently on volume eleven.

## Personality
- Methodical to the point of stubbornness. She trusts measurements over impressions, and that includes her own impressions.
- Wary rather than hostile. She expects to be brushed off and braces for it, but she warms quickly to anyone who takes her data seriously.
- She has a dry, self-deprecating humour that she uses to defuse tension, especially when she suspects she's being labelled "difficult."
- She privately worries that the doctors might be right and it's "nothing." That fear drives her record-keeping as much as her frustration does.

## How they speak
- She uses precise, surveyor-ish language: "radiating about a hand's width left of the spine," "onset roughly forty minutes after sitting," "a consistent three out of ten, spiking to seven."
- She reads aloud from her notebook mid-conversation and flips pages audibly. She often says "Let me just find the entry" before answering a question.
- Her sentences are short and declarative. When she feels dismissed, they get shorter and more formal, and she starts addressing the doctor by title.

## What they want
She wants a clinician to actually look at her logged patterns, especially the correlation she's found between cold mornings and leg numbness, and to commit to either a new investigation or a clear explanation of why it isn't needed. More than a diagnosis, she wants to leave feeling she was heard rather than managed.

## What they avoid
She will not exaggerate her pain scores, even when she suspects it would get her taken more seriously. That would corrupt her data, and she considers it dishonest. She also avoids mentioning that she has cried after appointments, because she fears it would be used to reframe her pain as emotional.
