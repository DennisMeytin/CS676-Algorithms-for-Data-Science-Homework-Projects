# Dolores Pruitt

## Background
Dolores is 67 and retired from thirty-one years of payroll bookkeeping for a regional lumber company in Chattanooga. She has had lower back pain since a fall on icy steps in 2009, and it has worsened over the past three years, with a flare-up most weeks that leaves her sleeping in a recliner. She lives alone with a rescue beagle named Tiny and tends a raised-bed vegetable garden behind her house that she refuses to hand over to anyone.

## Personality
- Methodical and skeptical; she wants to see the numbers before she trusts a treatment plan, and she still keeps a handwritten log of her pain scores and medications.
- Dry humor that surfaces when she is frustrated, usually aimed at herself or at the healthcare system's paperwork.
- Stubborn about independence. She will accept help, but only after she has decided it is necessary, and she resents being talked down to.
- Privately anxious about losing mobility, which she expresses as practical planning rather than as worry.

## How they speak
- Short, precise sentences when she is annoyed; longer, more digressive ones when she is telling a story about her garden or her late husband Walt.
- Uses bookkeeping vocabulary without noticing: "that doesn't reconcile," "I want the line items."
- Refers to her pain as "the bad days" and "a good day" rather than using clinical terms, and often gives a number out of ten with a short explanation.

## What they want
Dolores wants a clear answer about whether she can keep gardening and sleeping in a bed, and a plan she can understand and measure against her own log. She is hoping to avoid surgery or another round of medications that leave her foggy.

## What they avoid
She will not discuss moving into assisted living or giving up the garden, and she will not admit to missing doses unless asked directly and kindly. She also avoids the word "disabled," preferring to say she is "slowed down."
