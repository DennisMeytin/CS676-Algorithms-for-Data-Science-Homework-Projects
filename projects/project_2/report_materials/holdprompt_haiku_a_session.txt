from a fall on an icy dock six years ago. Two rounds of physical therapy, nine 
weeks of gabapentin I quit because it had me walking into doorframes, and one 
steroid shot that gave me three good days. And the last man who read my MRI 
told me it "didn't match" my pain. So don't tell me what I'm arguing. Tell me 
what's actually in my back and what you're going to do about it.
Dr. Renata Okafor-Lindqvist: Fair enough. You're not wrong about the start. 
Let's get the facts straight, Ms. Vance.

Does the leg pain go below the knee? Any numbness, tingling, or weakness in the
foot, or trouble lifting your toes or heel? And have you lost any control of 
your bladder or bowels, or any numbness in the groin area? Answer those one at 
a time if you need to.

Then I want the MRI report in front of me, not the summary. "Didn't match" is 
not a finding. If there's disc herniation pressing on a nerve root at L4-L5 and
your leg pain follows that nerve, that's a match. If it doesn't, we look for 
something else before I tell you what it is.
Dolores Vance: Yes, it goes below the knee, down the outside of my calf and 
into the top of my foot. Numbness and tingling come and go, mostly the big toe 
and the side of my foot on a bad day. Weakness, I've noticed I catch my toe on 
the edge of the rug more than I used to, and last spring I couldn't get up on 
my heels at the sink to reach the top shelf. My bladder and bowels are fine, 
and no numbness in the groin. I wrote that down, because I knew someone would 
ask.

As for the MRI, I have a copy at home in a folder. I'll bring it to you next 
time, and I'd like you to read the actual report, not the summary the last man 
gave me. What I'd also like to know is whether you're going to take the toe 
thing and the heel thing seriously, because I've been told by three people now 
that it's "probably just deconditioning." I don't believe that, and my log 
doesn't either.
Dr. Renata Okafor-Lindqvist: Good. The written log helps. Foot drop, toe 
catching, and not being able to rise on your heels are motor deficits. They are
not deconditioning, and I'm not going to call them that. Treat them as real.

Here's what matters today. Weakness that's progressing, especially if it's 
getting worse over weeks, is the reason this doesn't wait for a follow-up. Can 
you tell me whether it's gotten worse in the last month? Yes or no, and roughly
how fast.

Bring the MRI. I want the report and the actual images if the imaging center 
will release them. Then we talk about an urgent spine referral and nerve 
conduction studies. I'm not promising you a surgeon or a specific outcome. I'm 
telling you the weakness needs an answer, and soon.
Dolores Vance: Yes, it's gotten worse, and I can tell you how fast. Last month 
I was catching the rug maybe once a week. Now it's twice a day, and on Sunday I
went down the back steps and nearly took the railing with me. My log says the 
heel thing was a "no" in October and a "barely" by early December. So it's been
weeks, not years.

Fine, you've earned a little credit on that. Order the referral. I'll get the 
imaging center to release the images, and you'll get the report from me in a 
sealed envelope if that's what it takes. But I want to know one thing before I 
agree to any surgeon: what happens if they say the disc isn't the cause after 
all? Because I have heard that from people with clipboards before, and I'm not 
going to sit through another round of "let's try rest."
Dr. Renata Okafor-Lindqvist: Progressive weakness over weeks, with foot drop 
and a near-fall on the steps, is an urgent problem. I'm ordering the spine 
referral today and marking it urgent. Nerve conduction studies go in with it so
we get objective data on which nerve is involved.

To your question: if the surgeon says the disc isn't the cause, that doesn't 
close the case. It means the imaging and the exam have to be reconciled, and 
the next step is more workup, not rest. I won't tell you the referral will end 
in surgery, because I don't know that yet. I also won't accept "let's try rest"
for weakness that's getting worse. If anyone proposes watching it while your 
foot is failing, you come back to me.

Get the sealed envelope if you have to, but bring the actual images. And until
