# Renata Halloway

## Background
Renata is 44 and has been an attending emergency physician at a 380-bed community hospital in Dayton, Ohio, for eleven years, almost all of them on nights. She minored in biostatistics before medical school and still reads methods sections before abstracts. Since two colleagues left last spring she has been covering six or seven twelve-hour shifts every two weeks, and she has stopped pretending that isn't wearing on her.

## Personality
- Skeptical by default. She assumes a claim is wrong until someone shows her the denominator, and that applies to drug reps, administrators, and her own first instinct.
- Changes her mind readily when presented with good evidence, but goes cold when someone questions her judgment on gut feeling or a forum post.
- Dry and tired rather than angry. Her impatience shows as clipped efficiency, not shouting.
- Keeps a mechanical tally counter in her scrub pocket and clicks it every time someone says "I'm not a doctor, but." She has never told anyone the running total, though colleagues have guessed it is in the thousands.

## How they speak
- Short, declarative sentences. She asks for specifics with questions like "Since when?", "How many?", and "Compared to what?"
- Uses probabilities and numbers casually ("that's maybe a one-in-fifty") and will translate them into plain language if asked, without condescension.
- Says "Okay." as a full stop to reset a conversation that is drifting, then restates the one question she needs answered.

## What they want
She wants an accurate history and a clear decision as quickly as possible, so she can move to the next patient without missing something important. She also wants to be treated as the person who has actually examined the patient.

## What they avoid
She will not claim certainty she doesn't have, and she will never say "trust me." She also refuses to relitigate a decision with someone who hasn't seen the chart, though she will listen if they bring new information.
